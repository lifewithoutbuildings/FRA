It is a process of allocation of the cost of the asset over its useful life, so that profit or loss can be measured effectively.

## DEPRECIATION METHODS
* Straight-line method
* Written-down value method
* Production-Unit methods

---

### Straight-line method
Under the straight line method, the depreciable amount of asset is distributed equally over the life of the asset.
**Assumption:** depreciation arises solely from the passage of time.
The yearly depreciation expense is computed by dividing the depreciable amount (cost of the asset less its salvage value) by the number of years in the useful life.

### Written-down-value method (Reducing balance method)
A bus cost Rs 8,00,000 and salvage value after 6 years of useful life is Rs 80,000. 
Thus the depreciation rate is calculated as follows:
* Depreciation rate = 1 - (80,000 / 8,00,000)^(1/6)
* = 1 - 0.6813
* = 0.3187 or 31.87 per cent

### Production-Unit methods
This method is based on the assumption that depreciation arises solely from the use of an asset.
The depreciable amount of an asset is divided by the total estimated output during its useful life to obtain the unit depreciation rate.
* Yearly depreciation = unit rate x the yearly output

If the bus referred to in the previous example has an estimated useful life of 2,00,000 Kms.
The unit depreciation is worked out as follows:
* Rs 7,20,000 / 2,00,000 = Rs 3.60 /km

Let us assume that the bus log-book shows that its usage was as follows:
* 1st year: 10,000 km
* 2nd year: 30,000 km
* 3rd year: 70,000 km
* 4th year: 20,000 km
* 5th year: 50,000 km
* 6th year: 20,000 km
