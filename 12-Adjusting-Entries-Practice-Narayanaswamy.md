## Problem 4.15 — Navin Packaging Limited

> [!question] The case
> Navin Packaging Limited was set up on January 1, 20X3. Prepare the 20X5 balance sheet, statement of profit and loss and statement of changes in equity (no capital issued), plus adjusting and closing entries.

>
> | Account | Debit (₹) | Credit (₹) |
> |---|---:|---:|
> | Packaging equipment | 30,000 | |
> | Accumulated depreciation, packaging equipment | | 9,000 |
> | Office equipment | 10,000 | |
> | Accumulated depreciation, office equipment | | 3,000 |
> | Packaging supplies | 6,280 | |

> | Advertisement expense | 4,800 | |
> | Telephone expense | 1,200 | |
> | **Total** | **95,270** | **95,270** |
>
> **Adjustments:** (a) dep. packaging equip ₹3,000; (b) dep. office equip ₹1,000; (c) packaging supplies on hand ₹2,360; (d) office supplies on hand ₹1,190; (e) prepaid rent includes Jan–Mar 20X6 at ₹840/month; (f) accrued interest on bill payable @15% p.a. from Sept 1, 20X5; (g) advance-paid services earned ₹1,040; (h) unbilled revenue ₹1,390; (i) unpaid salaries ₹970; (j) prepaid advertisement ₹400; (k) unpaid telephone ₹200; (l) income tax ₹1,600.

### Adjusting entries

| Adj | Entry | Dr (₹) | Cr (₹) | Reasoning |
|---|---|---:|---:|---|
| (a) | Depreciation expense · To Accum. dep — packaging | 3,000 | 3,000 | Accum → 12,000; net 18,000 |
| (b) | Depreciation expense · To Accum. dep — office | 1,000 | 1,000 | Accum → 4,000; net 6,000 |
| (c) | Packaging supplies expense · To Packaging supplies | 3,920 | 3,920 | Consumed = 6,280 − 2,360 |
| (d) | Office supplies expense · To Office supplies | 1,460 | 1,460 | Consumed = 2,650 − 1,190 |
| (e) | Rent expense · To Prepaid rent | 7,080 | 7,080 | Expired = 9,600 − (3 × 840 = 2,520) |
| (f) | Interest expense · To Interest payable | 125 | 125 | 2,500 × 15% × 4/12 (Sep–Dec) |
| (g) | Unearned revenue · To Revenue | 1,040 | 1,040 | Advance now earned; unearned → 760 |
| (h) | Trade receivables · To Revenue | 1,390 | 1,390 | Accrued (unbilled) revenue |
| (i) | Salaries expense · To Salaries payable | 970 | 970 | Accrued salaries |
| (j) | Prepaid advertisement · To Advertisement expense | 400 | 400 | Advertisement paid in advance → asset |
| (k) | Telephone expense · To Telephone payable | 200 | 200 | Accrued telephone |
| (l) | Income tax expense · To Income tax payable | 1,600 | 1,600 | Estimated tax |

> [!note] The two "reverse-direction" adjustments (e) and (j)
> Most prepayment adjustments move an asset *into* an expense. In **(e)** rent, the expense is what expired (₹7,080) and the asset left is next year's ₹2,520. In **(j)** advertisement, the ₹4,800 was *already sitting in the expense account*, so we pull the ₹400 unexpired portion *back out* into a **Prepaid advertisement asset** — debit the asset, credit (reduce) the expense.

## Key takeaways

- Every service-company cycle runs the same five steps: **adjust → P&L → statement of changes in equity → balance sheet → close.**
- **Accumulated depreciation** accumulates: each year's charge adds to the prior balance; the asset shows at **cost − accumulated depreciation**.
- **Prepaid advertisement (4.15j)** and **prepaid rent (4.15e)** are pulled *out* of an expense already recorded — debit the asset, credit the expense.
- **Interest accrues by time:** 2,500 × 15% × 4/12 = **₹125** (Sept 1 → Dec 31).
- **Closing entries** zero out revenue, expenses and dividends into Retained earnings; balances tie — **4.14 ₹14,900**, **4.15 ₹42,850**, **4.16 July ₹29,555 / Aug ₹28,570**.
- In a two-period problem, **only balance-sheet items carry forward**; unpaid liabilities (income tax, bills) accumulate until settled.
