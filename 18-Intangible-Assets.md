> [!abstract] In one line
> Intangibles ask the same question as any asset — *does it buy future benefit?* — but because they're **invisible and easy to fake**, the rules are strict about what you may put on the books: **buy it → capitalise; grow it yourself → mostly expense.**

> [!info] Source
> Incorporated into the lecture notes (no separate handout). Built from [[FRA Session 11#7. Intangible Assets|S11 §7]] plus the **Fun Quiz** Q8 (brand) and Q9 (software) — both live examinable patterns.

---

## 1. The rules that matter (Ind AS 38)

> [!important]
> - **Purchased** intangible (a bought patent, licence, brand) → **capitalise** at cost.
> - **Internally generated** brand, customer list, masthead, **goodwill** → **cannot** be capitalised — too easy to invent a number. *(paras 48, 63)*
> - **Research** phase spending → **always expensed** (too speculative). **Development** phase → **capitalise** *only once the project is technically feasible and will sell.* *(paras 54, 57)*
> - **Finite life** → amortise (the intangible version of depreciation) over the **useful life**. **Indefinite life** (e.g. goodwill) → **not amortised**, but **impairment-tested annually**.

> [!warning] Amortise over USEFUL life, not the legal/copyright life
> If a copyright runs 40 years but management expects the product viable for only 5, you amortise over **5**. The shorter of legal and useful life governs — usually useful life.

## 2. Worked — Fun Quiz Q8 & Q9

> [!example] Q8 — purchased brand + internal spend to "strengthen" it
> XYZ bought a brand from ABC for **₹50 lakhs**, then spent a further **₹10 lakhs** to strengthen it. Carrying amount (ignore amortisation)?
>> **Purchased ₹50 lakhs → capitalise.** The extra ₹10 lakhs is **internally generated brand enhancement → expensed** (para 63). **Carrying amount = ₹50 lakhs.**
>> The trap: adding the ₹10 lakhs (→ ₹60 lakhs). You can capitalise a brand you **buy**, never the spend to build/boost it yourself.

> [!example] Q9 — research vs development, then amortise
> Software: research phase **$2,50,000**, development phase **$1,75,000**, copyright life 40 yrs, management-estimated viability **5 yrs**. Balance-sheet value after 1 year?
>> **Research $2,50,000 → expensed.** **Development $1,75,000 → capitalised.**
>> Amortise over the **useful life (5 yrs)**, not the 40-year copyright: 1,75,000/5 = $35,000.
>> Carrying amount after 1 year = 1,75,000 − 35,000 = **$1,40,000.**
>> Two traps in one: capitalising research (→ too high), and amortising over 40 years (→ ~$1,70,625).

## 3. Goodwill (S11 §7)

> [!note]
> Goodwill appears **only when you buy an entire business** for more than the fair value of its identifiable net assets:
> $$\text{Goodwill} = \text{Purchase price} - \text{Fair value of net assets acquired}$$
> Purchased goodwill is capitalised; **internally generated goodwill is never recognised**. Goodwill is **not amortised** — it is impairment-tested annually, and its impairment can **never be reversed**. A simple trade-in/asset swap creates **no goodwill** (you didn't buy a business).

---

## Traps

> [!warning]
> - **Capitalising internal brand-building / customer lists / internal goodwill** — expensed; only *purchased* intangibles go on the books.
> - **Capitalising research** — research is always expensed; only *development* (once feasible + saleable) is capitalised.
> - **Amortising over the legal/copyright life** when useful life is shorter — use the **useful life**.
> - **Amortising goodwill** — it isn't amortised; it's impairment-tested (and never reversed).

## Key takeaways

- Buy it → capitalise; build it yourself → mostly expense.
- Research **expensed**, development **capitalised** (when feasible & saleable); amortise over **useful life**.
- **Q8:** brand = **₹50 lakhs** (the ₹10 lakhs internal spend is expensed).
- **Q9:** software = **$1,40,000** (only development capitalised, amortised over 5 yrs).
- Goodwill = purchase price − FV of net assets; only *purchased*; not amortised; impairment-tested, never reversed.
