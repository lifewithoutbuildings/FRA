### Q2. Adjusting entries and final accounts (8 marks)

**Bright Tutorials Ltd** was set up on 1 January. Its **unadjusted trial balance at 31 January** is:

| Account | Debit (₹) | Credit (₹) |
|---|---:|---:|
| Equipment | 24,000 | |
| Supplies | 1,600 | |
| Prepaid insurance | 1,200 | |
| Cash | 3,400 | |
| Dividends | 500 | |
| Salaries expense | 2,000 | |
| Rent expense | 700 | |
| Trade payables | | 900 |
| Unearned tuition | | 2,400 |
| Share capital | | 20,000 |
| Tuition revenue | | 10,100 |
| **Total** | **33,400** | **33,400** |

Additional information at 31 January:
- (a) Equipment has a 5-year life and no salvage value.
- (b) Supplies on hand are ₹600.
- (c) The ₹1,200 insurance premium (paid 1 January) is for one year.
- (d) Of the unearned tuition, ₹900 has now been earned.
- (e) Salaries of ₹600 for the last few days are unpaid.
- (f) Tuition of ₹500 was provided to students but not yet billed.

**Required:** (i) the adjusting journal entries; (ii) the statement of profit and loss for January; (iii) the balance sheet at 31 January.

## Answer key

> [!success]- Q1 — debit/credit balances (4)
> a. **Credit** — accumulated depreciation is a *contra-asset*; it carries a credit balance. b. **Debit** — prepaid insurance is an asset. c. **Credit** — interest payable is a liability. d. **Debit** — drawings reduce equity and carry a debit balance. → [[10. Journal Ledger and Trial Balance#1. The rule that governs everything]]

> [!success]- Q2 — Bright Tutorials, full solution (8)
> **(i) Adjusting entries**
>
> | Adj | Entry | Dr (₹) | Cr (₹) |
> |---|---|---:|---:|
> | (a) | Depreciation expense · To Accumulated depreciation (24,000 ÷ 5 ÷ 12) | 400 | 400 |
> | (b) | Supplies expense · To Supplies (1,600 − 600) | 1,000 | 1,000 |
> | (c) | Insurance expense · To Prepaid insurance (1,200 ÷ 12) | 100 | 100 |
> | (d) | Unearned tuition · To Tuition revenue | 900 | 900 |
> | (e) | Salaries expense · To Salaries payable | 600 | 600 |
> | (f) | Trade receivables · To Tuition revenue | 500 | 500 |
>
> **(ii) Statement of profit and loss — January**
>
> | | ₹ |
> |---|---:|
> | Tuition revenue (10,100 + 900 + 500) | **11,500** |
> | Salaries (2,000 + 600) | (2,600) |
> | Supplies expense | (1,000) |
> | Rent | (700) |
> | Depreciation | (400) |
> | Insurance | (100) |
> | **Net profit** | **6,700** |
>
> *(Dividends ₹500 are **not** an expense — deducted from retained earnings: 6,700 − 500 = **6,200**.)*
>
> **(iii) Balance sheet — 31 January**
>
> | Assets | ₹ | Liabilities & Equity | ₹ |
> |---|---:|---|---:|
> | Equipment (24,000 − 400) | 23,600 | Trade payables | 900 |
> | Supplies | 600 | Unearned tuition (2,400 − 900) | 1,500 |
> | Prepaid insurance | 1,100 | Salaries payable | 600 |
> | Trade receivables | 500 | Share capital | 20,000 |
> | Cash | 3,400 | Retained earnings | 6,200 |
> | **Total** | **29,200** | **Total** | **29,200** |
>
> → [[11. Adjusting Entries and Final Accounts#1. Why adjusting entries exist]]

> [!success]- Q3 — omitted depreciation (1)
> **b. Assets overstated, equity overstated.** No accumulated depreciation recorded → **assets overstated**; no depreciation expense → profit and therefore **equity overstated**. → [[11. Adjusting Entries and Final Accounts#6. Depreciation via accumulated depreciation (Day 8)]]

> [!success]- Q4 — advance recorded as revenue (1)
> **a. Overstated by ₹50,000.** The ₹50,000 should sit in **unearned revenue** (a liability) until earned; booking it as revenue overstates income by the full amount. → [[11. Adjusting Entries and Final Accounts#1. Why adjusting entries exist]]

> [!success]- Q5 — prepaid rent (1)
> **b. Prepaid expense (an asset).** Cash paid before the benefit is consumed is an asset until it expires. → [[11. Adjusting Entries and Final Accounts#1. Why adjusting entries exist]]

> [!success]- Q6 — the table (4)
> **Year 1 (2023):** Retained earnings = revenue − expenses = (600 + 200) − 300 = **₹500 mn**. Liabilities = Assets − CC − RE = 2,000 − 400 − 500 = **₹1,100 mn**.
> **Year 2 (2024):** Profit = (1,800 + 700) − 1,900 = 600. Retained earnings = 500 + 600 − **50 dividends** = **₹1,050 mn**. Assets = L + CC + RE = 1,200 + 400 + 1,050 = **₹2,650 mn**.
>
> | | 2023 | 2024 |
> |---|---:|---:|
> | Assets | 2,000 | **2,650** |
> | Liabilities | **1,100** | 1,200 |
> | Retained earnings | **500** | **1,050** |
>
> → [[08. The Accounting Equation#2. The expanded equation — bringing in revenue and expenses]] · [[11. Adjusting Entries and Final Accounts#9. Statement of changes in equity]]

> [!success]- Q7 — zero opening balance (1)
> **c. Sales revenue.** Revenue is a temporary account, closed to zero at year-end and **restarting at ₹0**. Cash, accumulated depreciation and share capital are balance-sheet accounts that carry forward. → [[11. Adjusting Entries and Final Accounts#5. Closing entries (Day 8)]]

> | 3 | b |
> | 4 | a |

## The paper

### Q1. What goes into cost? (2 marks)
A firm buys a machine: invoice ₹4,80,000 (2% cash discount taken), freight ₹6,000, installation wages ₹9,000, installation overhead allocation ₹4,000, staff training ₹5,000, first month's electricity ₹3,000. **What is the capitalised cost?**

### Q2. Disposal (2 marks)
Equipment: gross cost ₹7,50,000, accumulated depreciation ₹5,10,000, sold for ₹1,80,000. **Journalise the disposal and state the gain/loss.**

### Q3. Change in method, SLM → WDV (3 marks)
Plant ₹6,00,000, residual ₹60,000, life 5 yrs, SLM. At the start of year 3 the firm switches to WDV, residual unchanged. **Compute the year-3 depreciation.**

### Q4. MCQ — capitalise or expense? (1 mark)
Which is **capital** expenditure?
a. Repainting the factory  b. Replacing a broken windowpane  c. Adding a mezzanine floor that raises capacity  d. Annual servicing

### Q5. Revaluation + depreciation (4 marks)
Building cost ₹10,00,000, life 20 yrs, residual nil, SLM. At the start of **year 5** it is revalued to ₹12,00,000 (residual still nil). **Compute: (a) carrying amount just before revaluation, (b) revaluation reserve created, (c) depreciation for year 5, (d) the annual reserve→general-reserve transfer.**

### Q6. Impairment (3 marks)
Machine carrying amount ₹40 lacs. Net selling price ₹28 lacs. Value in use ₹31 lacs. **(a) Recoverable amount? (b) Impairment loss? (c) If a revaluation reserve of ₹5 lacs exists for this machine, how is the loss split?**

### Q7. Exchange (2 marks)
A firm trades in an old **printer** (BV ₹80,000, FV ₹70,000) plus ₹1,50,000 cash for a new **printer** (invoice ₹2,40,000). **At what value is the new printer recorded, and why?**

### Q8. MCQ — intangibles (1 mark)
Which may be **capitalised**?
a. Internally generated goodwill  b. A purchased patent  c. Research costs  d. Cost of building an in-house brand

### Q9. MCQ — Delta/Singapore (1 mark)
An airline lengthens the depreciable life of its fleet and raises salvage %. The **immediate** effect on reported profit is:
a. lower  b. higher  c. unchanged  d. cannot say

### Q10. MCQ — impairment vs US GAAP (1 mark)
Under Ind AS, an impairment loss (not goodwill) that later reverses:
a. can never be reversed  b. may be reversed  c. is reversed only for land  d. reverses automatically

## Answer key (expand after attempting)

> [!success]- Q1 — **₹4,85,400**
> Invoice net of 2% = 4,80,000 × 0.98 = 4,70,400; + freight 6,000 + installation **wages** 9,000 = **₹4,85,400**. **Out:** overhead allocation (4,000), training (5,000), electricity (3,000) — general/running costs.

> [!success]- Q2 — **loss ₹60,000**
> NBV = 7,50,000 − 5,10,000 = 2,40,000; cash 1,80,000 → **loss 60,000**.
> ```
> Dr Cash 1,80,000 / Dr Accum.Dep 5,10,000 / Dr Loss 60,000 → Cr Equipment 7,50,000
> ```

> [!success]- Q3 — **₹1,77,174**
> SLM dep = (6,00,000 − 60,000)/5 = 1,08,000/yr → 2 yrs → accumulated 2,16,000 → carrying **3,84,000**, remaining life 3 yrs.
> WDV rate (from carrying amount, remaining life) = 1 − (60,000/3,84,000)^(1/3) = **0.4614 (46.14%)**.
> Year-3 dep = 3,84,000 × 0.4614 ≈ **₹1,77,174**. *(Using the cost-based rate 1−(60,000/6,00,000)^(1/5)=36.9% would be **wrong** — that's the whole trap.)*

> [!success]- Q4 — **c** (mezzanine raises capacity = capital). a, b, d are repairs/maintenance = revenue expenditure.

> [!success]- Q5 — (a) **₹8,40,000** (b) **₹3,60,000** (c) **₹75,000** (d) **₹25,000**
> Annual SLM dep = 10,00,000/20 = 50,000. After 4 yrs accumulated 2,00,000 → **carrying 8,40,000** (a).
> Revalue to 12,00,000 → **reserve = 12,00,000 − 8,40,000 = 3,60,000** (b).
> Remaining life 16 yrs → new dep = 12,00,000/16 = **75,000** (c).
> Excess depreciation = 75,000 − 50,000 = **25,000** transferred reserve→general reserve each year (d).

> [!success]- Q6 — (a) **₹31 lacs** (b) **₹9 lacs** (c) ₹5 lacs to reserve, **₹4 lacs to P&L**
> Recoverable = max(28, 31) = **31**; impairment = 40 − 31 = **9**. With a ₹5-lac reserve: reserve absorbs 5 (OCI), remaining **4 → P&L**.

> [!success]- Q7 — **₹2,30,000**
> Similar assets (printer for printer) → **no commercial substance** → **BV + cash** = 80,000 + 1,50,000 = **₹2,30,000**; no gain/loss; fair value and invoice ignored.

> [!success]- Q8 — **b** (purchased patent). Internal goodwill, research, in-house brand are all expensed.

> [!success]- Q9 — **b** (higher). Longer life + higher salvage → lower annual depreciation → higher reported profit now (the Delta move).

> [!success]- Q10 — **b** (may be reversed under Ind AS; goodwill never; US GAAP never).
