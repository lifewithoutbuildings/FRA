## Topic 03/04 — The Balance Sheet

> [!question] Q3.1 (conceptual)
> Which is the single defining characteristic common to *all* assets: (a) tangibility, (b) long life, (c) future economic benefit, (d) physical existence?
>> [!success]- Solution
>> **(c) future economic benefit.** Tangibility and long life fail for intangibles and current assets respectively. (Quiz 1 Q17.) → [[03-Balance-Sheet-Assets#What is an asset?]]

> [!question] C3.2 (computational)
> A machine costs ₹5,00,000, has an estimated residual value of ₹50,000 and a useful life of 5 years (straight-line). Find (i) annual depreciation, (ii) depreciation for a machine bought on 1 October in a year ending 31 March, (iii) net block after the first (part-)year.
>> [!success]- Solution
>> (i) $(5,00,000 - 50,000)/5 = \textbf{₹90,000/yr}$.
>> (ii) In use Oct–Mar = 6 months: $90,000 \times 6/12 = \textbf{₹45,000}$.
>> (iii) Net block $= 5,00,000 - 45,000 = \textbf{₹4,55,000}$. → [[90-Master-Formula-Sheet#C · Fixed Assets & Depreciation]]

> [!question] Q3.3 (conceptual)
> A firm's identical plot of land, bought years ago for ₹10 lakh, is now worth ₹40 lakh. At what value does it appear on the balance sheet, and which concept governs this?
>> [!success]- Solution
>> **₹10 lakh** — the **historical cost** concept. No write-up on a mere change in market value (and land isn't depreciated). → [[07-Conceptual-Framework-and-Core-Concepts#Historical Cost Concept]]

> [!question] C3.4 (computational)
> Company P acquires 100% of Company S for ₹90 cr. S's identifiable net assets have a fair value of ₹65 cr. Compute goodwill. If P had instead paid ₹60 cr, what appears?
>> [!success]- Solution
>> Goodwill $= 90 - 65 = \textbf{₹25 cr}$. At ₹60 cr, purchase price < fair value of net assets → a **bargain purchase / capital reserve** (negative goodwill), not an asset. → [[03-Balance-Sheet-Assets#Goodwill]]

> [!question] Q3.5 (conceptual)
> Rank Lenders, Equity shareholders and Preference shareholders by (i) priority of payment and (ii) risk borne.
>> [!success]- Solution
>> **Priority:** Lenders → Preference → Equity. **Risk (highest first):** Equity → Preference → Lenders. Priority and risk run in **opposite** order. → [[04-Balance-Sheet-Equity-and-Liabilities#Providers of capital — the claim hierarchy]]

> [!question] C3.6 (computational)
> A company issues 2,00,000 shares of ₹10 face value at ₹35 each. Split the proceeds between Equity Share Capital and Securities Premium, and give the journal entry.
>> [!success]- Solution
>> Share capital $= 10 \times 2,00,000 = \textbf{₹20,00,000}$; Premium $= 25 \times 2,00,000 = \textbf{₹50,00,000}$.
>> ```
>> Bank A/c                 Dr. 70,00,000
>>     To Equity Share Capital A/c   20,00,000
>>     To Securities Premium A/c     50,00,000
>> ```
>> → [[04-Balance-Sheet-Equity-and-Liabilities#Share capital vs premium]]

> [!question] Q3.7 (conceptual)
> Distinguish a **provision** from a **contingent liability**. A firm faces a lawsuit it will *probably* lose, with damages reliably estimable at ₹5 cr. How is it treated?
>> [!success]- Solution
>> A **provision** is recognised (probable + estimable); a **contingent liability** is only disclosed. Here — probable **and** estimable → **recognise a ₹5 cr provision** (charge to P&L, liability on B/S). → [[04-Balance-Sheet-Equity-and-Liabilities#Contingent liabilities]]

## Topic 05 — The Income Statement

> [!question] C5.1 (computational — the inventory→COGS→tax chain)
> Revenue ₹3,000; production cost incurred ₹1,400; opening FG ₹200; closing FG ₹350; tax rate 30%. Find the changes-in-inventory line, COGS, gross profit, and PAT.
>> [!success]- Solution
>> Changes in inventory $= 200 - 350 = \textbf{(150)}$ → reduces expense.
>> COGS $= 1,400 - 150 = \textbf{₹1,250}$. Gross profit $= 3,000 - 1,250 = \textbf{₹1,750}$.
>> Tax $= 1,750 \times 0.30 = 525$; PAT $= 1,750 - 525 = \textbf{₹1,225}$.
>> Note the ₹150 of unsold production sits as **closing inventory (an asset)** on the balance sheet. → [[05-Income-Statement-PandL#The "Changes in inventories" line]]

> [!question] C5.2 (computational — basic EPS)
> PAT ₹50,00,000; preference dividend ₹2,00,000. Shares: 8,00,000 outstanding all year, plus 3,00,000 issued on 1 January (year ends 31 March). Find Basic EPS.
>> [!success]- Solution
>> Weighted shares $= 8,00,000 + 3,00,000 \times (3/12) = 8,75,000$.
>> Numerator $= 50,00,000 - 2,00,000 = 48,00,000$.
>> Basic EPS $= 48,00,000 / 8,75,000 = \textbf{₹5.49}$. → [[05-Income-Statement-PandL#Basic EPS]]

> [!question] C5.3 (computational — diluted EPS)
> Continue C5.2. The firm has convertible debentures: annual interest ₹4,00,000, tax 30%, convertible into 1,25,000 shares. Find Diluted EPS and confirm it is dilutive.
>> [!success]- Solution
>> Numerator $= 48,00,000 + 4,00,000 \times (1-0.30) = 48,00,000 + 2,80,000 = 50,80,000$.
>> Denominator $= 8,75,000 + 1,25,000 = 10,00,000$.
>> Diluted EPS $= 50,80,000 / 10,00,000 = \textbf{₹5.08}$. Since ₹5.08 < ₹5.49 → **dilutive** (correctly included). → [[05-Income-Statement-PandL#Diluted EPS]]

> [!question] Q5.4 (conceptual)
> HUL reports profit of ₹15,059 cr but profit from continuing operations of ₹10,652 cr. Which figure better reflects sustainable performance, and why?
>> [!success]- Solution
>> **Continuing operations (₹10,652 cr).** The ₹15,059 headline is inflated by a **one-time, non-cash demerger gain** in discontinued operations — not repeatable operating performance. Always strip exceptional/discontinued items. → [[05-Income-Statement-PandL#Below the operating line]]

> [!question] Q5.5 (conceptual)
> Why is diluted EPS never higher than basic EPS?
>> [!success]- Solution
>> Any potential share that would *raise* EPS is **anti-dilutive** and excluded by rule; only dilutive (EPS-lowering) instruments are added. So diluted ≤ basic. → [[05-Income-Statement-PandL#Diluted EPS]]

## Topic 06 — Cash Flow

> [!question] C6.1 (computational)
> PBT ₹1,200; depreciation ₹300; gain on sale of machine ₹80; receivables ↑ ₹150; inventory ↓ ₹60; payables ↑ ₹90; tax paid ₹250. Compute operating cash flow (indirect method).
>> [!success]- Solution
>> $1,200 + 300 - 80 - 150 + 60 + 90 - 250 = \textbf{₹1,170}$.
>> (Add back non-cash dep'n; remove the gain (non-operating); receivables ↑ subtract; inventory ↓ add; payables ↑ add; less tax.) → [[06-Cash-Flow-Statement#The indirect method]]

> [!question] Q6.2 (conceptual)
> A firm reports a healthy profit but its cash fell sharply. Give three distinct reasons this can happen.
>> [!success]- Solution
>> (1) Sales on **credit** (receivables up, cash not collected); (2) **capital expenditure**/acquisitions and **dividends** (drain cash, bypass the P&L); (3) profit contained **non-cash gains** (e.g. asset-sale or demerger gains) or was reduced by non-cash depreciation. → [[06-Cash-Flow-Statement#Why a separate cash statement?]] (see Maria Hernandez & HUL)

## Topic 07 — Concepts

> [!question] Q7.1 (conceptual — the accrual timing trap)
> On 31 March 2024 (year-end), a firm (i) pays April rent in advance, and (ii) has used March electricity for which the bill arrives in April. State the effect of each on FY2023-24 **profit**.
>> [!success]- Solution
>> (i) **No effect** — April rent is a **prepaid asset**; benefit belongs to next year. (ii) **Decrease** — March electricity is a **March expense** (accrue it), regardless of when paid. Cash timing ≠ expense timing. (Quiz 1 Q8/Q9.) → [[07-Conceptual-Framework-and-Core-Concepts#Accrual Concept]]

> [!question] Q7.2 (conceptual)
> A ₹40 stapler is expensed rather than capitalised as an asset. Which concept, and why not "conservatism"?
>> [!success]- Solution
>> **Materiality** — the amount is too small to affect any user's decision, so full asset-tracking isn't warranted. Conservatism is about anticipating losses, not about size. (Quiz 1 Q14.) → [[07-Conceptual-Framework-and-Core-Concepts#Materiality Concept]]

> [!question] Q7.3 (conceptual)
> Why do equity and liabilities appear on the *same* side of the balance sheet? Name the governing concept.
>> [!success]- Solution
>> Both are **sources of funds** (claims on assets); the **separate entity concept** means the owner's contribution is an amount the business *owes back* to them, hence a claim like a liability. → [[07-Conceptual-Framework-and-Core-Concepts#Separate Entity (Business Entity) Concept]]

## Topic 08 — The Accounting Equation

> [!question] C8.1 (computational — the flagship type, cf. Quiz 1 Q1)
> Opening retained earnings ₹9,000; contributed capital ₹8,000; liabilities ₹14,000; revenue ₹25,000; expenses ₹19,000; dividends ₹2,000. Find total assets.
>> [!success]- Solution
>> Closing RE $= 9,000 + (25,000 - 19,000) - 2,000 = 13,000$.
>> Equity $= 8,000 + 13,000 = 21,000$. Assets $= L + E = 14,000 + 21,000 = \textbf{35,000}$.
>> → [[08-The-Accounting-Equation#2. The expanded equation — bringing in revenue and expenses]]

> [!question] C8.2 (computational — transaction effects)
> For each, state the two accounts and directions: (a) buy machinery for cash; (b) receive cash from a debtor; (c) rent becomes due but is unpaid; (d) owner withdraws cash.
>> [!success]- Solution
>> (a) Machinery ↑ / Cash ↓ (asset swap — total assets unchanged). (b) Cash ↑ / Debtors ↓ (asset swap). (c) Rent expense ↑ (equity ↓) / Rent payable ↑ (liability). (d) Cash ↓ / Owner's equity ↓ (drawings). (Quiz 1 Q20, Q12, Q7, Q18.) → [[08-The-Accounting-Equation#3. Transaction-effect analysis — the core skill]]

> [!question] C8.3 (computational — Music-Mart style)
> A store sells goods costing ₹1,200 for ₹1,800 on credit. State the full set of effects.
>> [!success]- Solution
>> Accounts receivable ↑ **1,800**; Inventory ↓ **1,200**; Retained earnings ↑ **600** (profit = 1,800 − 1,200). The "double dual aspect": revenue side **and** cost side recorded together. → [[08-The-Accounting-Equation#Worked case — Music Mart, Inc.]]

> [!question] C8.4 (computational — closing capital)
> Opening capital ₹6,00,000; profit ₹1,20,000; drawings ₹80,000; fresh capital introduced ₹50,000. Find closing capital.
>> [!success]- Solution
>> $6,00,000 + 1,20,000 - 80,000 + 50,000 = \textbf{₹6,90,000}$. → [[90-Master-Formula-Sheet#A · The Accounting Equation → 08-The-Accounting-Equation]]

## Topic 09 — The Accounting Cycle

> [!question] C9.1 (computational — adjusting entries)
> At year-end: (a) ₹8,00,000 loan at 9% outstanding all year, no interest paid; (b) machine cost ₹6,00,000, life 10 yrs, no residual, straight-line; (c) prepaid insurance of ₹24,000 for the year, of which 3 months remain. Give each adjusting entry and amount.
>> [!success]- Solution
>> (a) Interest $= 8,00,000 \times 9\% = 72,000$: **Dr Interest exp / Cr Interest payable 72,000**.
>> (b) Depreciation $= 6,00,000/10 = 60,000$: **Dr Depreciation / Cr Accumulated dep'n 60,000**.
>> (c) Expired insurance $= 24,000 \times 9/12 = 18,000$: **Dr Insurance exp / Cr Prepaid insurance 18,000** (₹6,000 remains an asset). → [[09-The-Accounting-Cycle-Recording-Transactions#4. Adjusting entries — the "implicit transactions"]]

> [!question] Q9.2 (conceptual — DEAD CLIC)
> State whether each is a debit or credit to record the increase: (a) sales revenue, (b) machinery, (c) a bank loan taken, (d) rent expense.
>> [!success]- Solution
>> (a) Revenue ↑ → **Credit**. (b) Asset ↑ → **Debit**. (c) Liability ↑ → **Credit**. (d) Expense ↑ → **Debit**. → [[09-The-Accounting-Cycle-Recording-Transactions#2. Debit and credit — the rules]]

> [!question] C9.3 (computational — profit ≠ cash, Maria-style)
> A consultancy earns ₹60,000 revenue (₹15,000 still receivable), pays ₹38,000 cash expenses, records ₹4,000 depreciation, and buys ₹10,000 of equipment for cash. Compute (i) profit and (ii) the change in cash.
>> [!success]- Solution
>> (i) Profit $= 60,000 - 38,000 - 4,000 = \textbf{18,000}$.
>> (ii) Cash change $= (60,000 - 15,000\ \text{received}) - 38,000 - 10,000 = 45,000 - 48,000 = \textbf{−3,000}$.
>> Profitable yet cash fell — receivables, capex, and non-cash depreciation explain the gap. → [[09-The-Accounting-Cycle-Recording-Transactions#5. Worked case — Maria Hernandez & Associates]]

> [!question] C9.4 (computational — cash to accrual)
> Cash collected from customers ₹80,000; closing receivables ₹12,000 (opening nil). Cash paid to suppliers ₹50,000; closing payables ₹8,000 (opening nil); opening inventory ₹9,000, closing inventory ₹7,000. Find (i) revenue and (ii) COGS.
>> [!success]- Solution
>> (i) Revenue $= 80,000 + 12,000 = \textbf{₹92,000}$.
>> (ii) Purchases $= 50,000 + 8,000 = 58,000$; COGS $= 9,000 + 58,000 - 7,000 = \textbf{₹60,000}$. → [[09-The-Accounting-Cycle-Recording-Transactions#Lone Pine (B) — Income statement, 1 Nov 2009 – 30 Mar 2010]]

## Quiz 2 (Sessions 5–6) — MCQ practice

> [!info] Scope
> Multiple-choice drills on the **recording machinery, adjusting entries, closing entries, depreciation and inventory systems** from [[10-Journal-Ledger-and-Trial-Balance]], [[11-Adjusting-Entries-and-Final-Accounts]] and [[12-Adjusting-Entries-Practice-Narayanaswamy]]. Pick an answer, then expand to check.

### Recording — journal, ledger, trial balance

> [!question] QM1 — Which account **increases with a debit**?
> a) Sales revenue  b) Bank loan  c) Prepaid insurance  d) Share capital
>> [!success]- Answer
>> **(c) Prepaid insurance** — an asset, so it increases on the debit side. Revenue, loans and share capital are all credit-increase (DEAD CLIC). → [[10-Journal-Ledger-and-Trial-Balance#1. The rule that governs everything]]

> [!question] QM2 — A firm buys inventory **on credit**. The correct journal entry is:
> a) Dr Inventory / Cr Cash  b) Dr Inventory / Cr Accounts payable  c) Dr Accounts payable / Cr Inventory  d) Dr Cash / Cr Inventory
>> [!success]- Answer
>> **(b)** Inventory (asset ↑) is debited; the supplier is owed, so Accounts payable (liability ↑) is credited. → [[10-Journal-Ledger-and-Trial-Balance#Step 1 — Journal entries]]

> [!question] QM3 — Under a **perpetual** system, selling goods costing ₹350 for ₹550 on credit affects how many ledger accounts in total?
> a) 1  b) 2  c) 3  d) 4
>> [!success]- Answer
>> **(d) 4.** The revenue side (Dr Debtors / Cr Sales) *and* the cost side (Dr COGS / Cr Inventory) are both recorded — the "double dual aspect" of a sale. → [[10-Journal-Ledger-and-Trial-Balance#Step 1 — Journal entries]]

> [!question] QM4 — In the ten-transaction case, the ₹250 of unsold goods appears in the trial balance as:
> a) a credit balance  b) a debit balance  c) it does not appear  d) a P&L item
>> [!success]- Answer
>> **(b) a debit balance** — closing inventory is an **asset**. (Under a *periodic* system it would instead be a year-end adjustment, not a ledger balance.) → [[10-Journal-Ledger-and-Trial-Balance#Step 3 — Trial Balance]]

> [!question] QM5 — A trial balance whose two columns are **equal** proves that:
> a) there are no errors of any kind  b) the books are arithmetically balanced  c) every transaction is classified correctly  d) profit is correct
>> [!success]- Answer
>> **(b).** A tally only confirms total Dr = total Cr; it misses wrong-account, omission and compensating errors. → [[10-Journal-Ledger-and-Trial-Balance#2. The three records — journal, ledger, trial balance]]

> [!question] QM6 — Cash has a "By balance c/d ₹370" on its credit side. Next period it opens as:
> a) ₹370 credit b/d  b) ₹370 debit b/d  c) nil  d) ₹370 in the P&L
>> [!success]- Answer
>> **(b) ₹370 debit b/d.** Cash is debit-natured, so its closing balance reopens on the debit side. → [[10-Journal-Ledger-and-Trial-Balance#Balancing off an account — "balance c/d" and "balance b/d"]]

> [!question] QM7 — In the ten-transaction case (Sales 550, COGS 350, general expenses 80), gross profit and net profit are:
> a) 200 and 120  b) 120 and 200  c) 200 and 200  d) 470 and 120
>> [!success]- Answer
>> **(a) 200 and 120.** Gross profit = 550 − 350 = 200; net profit = 200 − 80 = 120. → [[10-Journal-Ledger-and-Trial-Balance#4. Trading & P&L account and Balance Sheet]]

### Adjusting entries

> [!question] QM8 — Staff salaries earned but **unpaid** at year-end require:
> a) Dr Salaries payable / Cr Salaries expense  b) Dr Salaries expense / Cr Salaries payable  c) no entry until paid  d) Dr Cash / Cr Salaries expense
>> [!success]- Answer
>> **(b)** — an accrued expense: expense ↑ (debit), liability ↑ (credit). → [[11-Adjusting-Entries-and-Final-Accounts#1. Why adjusting entries exist]]

> [!question] QM9 — A club held ₹2,100 unearned; ₹300 of it has now been earned. The adjusting entry:
> a) Dr Revenue 300 / Cr Unearned revenue 300  b) Dr Unearned revenue 300 / Cr Revenue 300  c) Dr Cash 300 / Cr Revenue 300  d) Dr Unearned revenue 2,100 / Cr Revenue 2,100
>> [!success]- Answer
>> **(b).** Only the ₹300 earned moves out of the liability into revenue; ₹1,800 stays unearned. → [[11-Adjusting-Entries-and-Final-Accounts#3. Capstone case — Leisure Centre Ltd (Problem 4.12)]]

> [!question] QM10 — Services worth ₹1,200 performed but **not yet billed** call for:
> a) Dr Revenue / Cr Receivables  b) Dr Receivables / Cr Revenue  c) no entry until billed  d) Dr Cash / Cr Revenue
>> [!success]- Answer
>> **(b)** — accrued (unbilled) revenue: asset ↑, revenue ↑. Accrual recognises income when earned, not when billed. → [[11-Adjusting-Entries-and-Final-Accounts#1. Why adjusting entries exist]]

> [!question] QM11 — An asset costing ₹12,000, 5-year life, no salvage, has run **one month**. Depreciation for the period:
> a) ₹2,400  b) ₹1,000  c) ₹200  d) ₹100
>> [!success]- Answer
>> **(c) ₹200.** 12,000 ÷ 5 = 2,400/yr; ÷ 12 = **₹200/month**. Always pro-rate to the period. → [[11-Adjusting-Entries-and-Final-Accounts#3. Capstone case — Leisure Centre Ltd (Problem 4.12)]]

> [!question] QM12 — Dividends of ₹800 are:
> a) an operating expense in the P&L  b) a distribution deducted from retained earnings  c) revenue  d) a contra-asset
>> [!success]- Answer
>> **(b).** Dividends are a distribution of profit to owners, never a P&L expense. → [[11-Adjusting-Entries-and-Final-Accounts#3. Capstone case — Leisure Centre Ltd (Problem 4.12)]]

### Depreciation, closing entries & inventory

> [!question] QM13 — "Accumulated depreciation" is best classified as:
> a) an expense  b) a contra-asset  c) a liability  d) equity
>> [!success]- Answer
>> **(b) a contra-asset** — it reduces the asset's carrying value while keeping original cost visible. → [[11-Adjusting-Entries-and-Final-Accounts#6. Depreciation via accumulated depreciation (Day 8)]]

> [!question] QM14 — Packaging equipment cost ₹30,000; accumulated depreciation is ₹12,000. Net book value:
> a) ₹30,000  b) ₹27,000  c) ₹21,000  d) ₹18,000
>> [!success]- Answer
>> **(d) ₹18,000.** Carrying amount = cost − accumulated depreciation = 30,000 − 12,000. → [[11-Adjusting-Entries-and-Final-Accounts#6. Depreciation via accumulated depreciation (Day 8)]]

> [!question] QM15 — The purpose of **closing entries** is to:
> a) correct posting errors  b) reset revenue and expense accounts to zero and transfer the result to retained earnings  c) prepare the trial balance  d) record depreciation
>> [!success]- Answer
>> **(b).** Temporary accounts are zeroed each year; only their net effect (profit) lands in retained earnings. → [[11-Adjusting-Entries-and-Final-Accounts#5. Closing entries (Day 8)]]

> [!question] QM16 — On the **first day of the new year**, which account opens with a non-zero balance?
> a) Sales  b) Salaries expense  c) Cash  d) Depreciation expense
>> [!success]- Answer
>> **(c) Cash** — a balance-sheet account carries forward. Sales, salaries and depreciation are temporary and reopen at **₹0**. → [[11-Adjusting-Entries-and-Final-Accounts#5. Closing entries (Day 8)]]

> [!question] QM17 — Under the **periodic** system, goods bought for resale go to a ______ account, whose nature is an ______:
> a) Inventory; asset  b) Purchases; expense  c) COGS; asset  d) Purchases; asset
>> [!success]- Answer
>> **(b) Purchases; expense.** At year-end the unsold closing stock is pulled out as an asset; the remainder is COGS. → [[11-Adjusting-Entries-and-Final-Accounts#7. Recording inventory — perpetual vs periodic (Day 8)]]

> [!question] QM18 — Opening stock ₹40,000; purchases ₹1,55,000; closing stock ₹25,000. COGS =
> a) ₹1,20,000  b) ₹1,70,000  c) ₹1,80,000  d) ₹1,95,000
>> [!success]- Answer
>> **(b) ₹1,70,000.** COGS = 40,000 + 1,55,000 − 25,000. → [[11-Adjusting-Entries-and-Final-Accounts#7. Recording inventory — perpetual vs periodic (Day 8)]]

> [!question] QM19 — Which cost is **capitalised** rather than expensed?
> a) routine machine servicing  b) repainting the office  c) a renovation that extends a building's useful life  d) replacing a cracked window pane
>> [!success]- Answer
>> **(c).** A renovation that enhances/extends the asset is capital expenditure; repairs and maintenance are revenue expenditure (expensed). → [[11-Adjusting-Entries-and-Final-Accounts#8. Capital vs revenue expenditure — repairs vs renovation (Day 8)]]

## Mixed / integrative

> [!question] CX.1 (integrative — build mini-statements)
> A trader starts with ₹1,00,000 cash (owner's capital). Buys goods ₹60,000 on credit; sells half of them (cost ₹30,000) for ₹50,000 cash; pays ₹5,000 rent; ₹2,000 rent for next period is prepaid. Prepare the closing balance sheet.
>> [!success]- Solution
>> Profit $= 50,000 - 30,000 - 5,000 = 15,000$. Cash $= 1,00,000 + 50,000 - 5,000 - 2,000 = 1,43,000$.
>> | Assets | ₹ | L + E | ₹ |
>> |---|---:|---|---:|
>> | Cash | 1,43,000 | Payables | 60,000 |
>> | Inventory | 30,000 | Capital (1,00,000 + 15,000) | 1,15,000 |
