Revaluation 

➢ Revaluation: Either on Gross basis or on Net basis. 

The current book value has to be compared with the book value on the basis of the revalued amount. 

The difference in book values represents REVALUATION RESERVE. This is added to the equity directly in the Balance Sheet and not routed through P&L Account. 

The Revaluation Reserve changes from year to year for two reasons:

a. The excess depreciation due to revaluation. This excess may be reduced from Revaluation Reserve and transferred to General Reserve. The distributable profits, therefore, remain unaffected. 

b. The revaluation done from time to time - Revaluations on higher side increases revaluation reserve and revaluation on the downside reduces revaluation reserve. 

On disposal of the asset, the balance in Revaluation Reserve is transferred to General Reserve.

Day 4

➢ Impairment: It’s a one-time reduction in the value of the asset due to obsolescence or major damage etc. 

How to find out whether impairment has occurred? 

Compare book value with Recoverable amount. 

If recoverable amount is less than book value, impairment has happened. 

What is recoverable amount?

Higher of fair disposal value and value in use. 

Impairment losses are routed through P&L A/C, and assets are reduced. 

However, if revaluation Reserve exists, then impairment loss is recovered from Revaluation Reserve first, and then the excess is routed through P&L A/C.
